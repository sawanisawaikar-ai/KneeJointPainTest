# Chronic Knee Pain: Evidence-Based Review Summary

## Overview

This is an evidence-based review on chronic knee pain, specifically focusing on **osteoarthritis (OA)** of the knee. The paper was published in *Pain Practice* (2025) and represents a comprehensive guide for interventional pain medicine.

**Authors:** Thibaut Vanneste, Amy Belba, Gezina T. M. L. Oei, Pieter Emans, Loic Fonkoue, Jan Willem Kallewaard, Leonardo Kapural, Philip Peng, Michael Sommer, Bert Vanneste, Steven P. Cohen, Jan Van Zundert

**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1111/papr.13408

## Definition

- **Chronic knee pain** is defined as knee pain that persists or recurs for more than **3 months**
- The most common cause is **degenerative osteoarthritis (OA)**

---

## Epidemiology

- Global prevalence of knee OA: **16.0%** in individuals aged 15+ and **22.9%** in those aged 40+
- Global incidence: **203 per 10,000 person-years** in individuals aged 20+
- Both prevalence and incidence **increase with age**
- Approximately **50%** of patients with symptomatic knee OA eventually require total knee arthroplasty (TKA)
- **10-35%** of TKA patients experience persistent pain post-surgery

---

## Risk Factors

- Advanced age (especially >55 years)
- Obesity
- Female sex
- Previous knee injury
- Occupational factors (~10-15% attributed to trauma)

---

## Pathophysiology

OA is a degenerative condition causing:
- Progressive loss of articular cartilage
- Remodeling of underlying bone, fibrocartilage, and synovium
- Can affect all three compartments of the knee:
  - Medial tibiofemoral
  - Lateral tibiofemoral
  - Patellofemoral

OA results from an interplay of **biomechanical, inflammatory, and metabolic factors** contributing to cartilage damage, synovitis, and subchondral bone abnormalities.

---

## Pain Characteristics

- **Predominantly nociceptive** pain
- **Nociplastic characteristics** in ~35% of patients
- **Neuropathic qualities** occasionally present in severely diseased knees
- Pain is usually mechanical, worsening with weight-bearing activity
- Flare-ups can last up to 72 hours with swelling, warmth, and worsening pain

---

## Diagnosis

### History
- Insidious, chronic, gradually worsening knee pain
- Reduced knee function
- Muscle weakness
- Morning stiffness
- Joint instability
- Psychological distress

### Physical Examination
- Standard musculoskeletal and neurological examination
- Compare diseased knee to contralateral knee
- Inspect for redness, edema, muscle atrophy
- Palpate for bony enlargements, crepitus, tenderness
- Evaluate gait and stance

### Imaging
- While typical clinical findings are sufficient for diagnosis, medical imaging may improve specificity
- X-rays, MRI can be used to confirm OA and assess severity

### Differential Diagnosis
- Rheumatoid arthritis
- Spondylo-arthropathies
- Crystal arthropathies
- Infections
- Post-traumatic pain
- Persistent postsurgical pain (PPSP)

---

## Treatment Options

### Conservative Treatments
- Physical therapy
- Weight management
- Exercise
- analgesics (NSAIDs, acetaminophen)

### Interventional Treatments

| Treatment | Recommendation |
|-----------|----------------|
| Intra-articular corticosteroid injections | **Recommended** |
| Conventional radiofrequency of genicular nerves | **Recommended** |
| Cooled radiofrequency of genicular nerves | **Recommended** |
| Intra-articular hyaluronic acid injections | **Conditionally recommended** |
| Platelet-rich plasma (PRP) injections | Not enough evidence (study setting only) |
| Chemical ablation of genicular nerves | Not enough evidence (study setting only) |
| Neurostimulation (PNS, DRG stimulation) | Not enough evidence (study setting only) |

### Surgical Options
- Joint-preserving procedures
- Total knee arthroplasty (TKA) - for end-stage OA

---

## Key Recommendations

1. **Multidisciplinary approach** is essential when conservative measures fail
2. **Intra-articular corticosteroid injections** and **radiofrequency ablation** of genicular nerves are effective interventional options
3. **Hyaluronic acid** injections are conditionally recommended
4. **PRP, chemical ablation, and neurostimulation** require more evidence before routine recommendation
5. Decision for joint-preserving vs. joint-replacement surgery should be made multidisciplinary

## Treatment Algorithm (Summary)

```
Chronic Knee Pain (3+ months)
        ↓
Conservative Treatment (PT, weight loss, medications)
        ↓
If unsuccessful → Intra-articular corticosteroid injections
        ↓
If unsuccessful → Radiofrequency ablation of genicular nerves
        ↓
If unsuccessful → Consider:
  - Hyaluronic acid (conditional)
  - PRP (study setting)
  - Surgical evaluation
```

---

## Conclusion

This evidence-based review provides comprehensive guidance for managing chronic knee pain due to osteoarthritis. When conservative treatments fail, a multidisciplinary approach including psychological therapy, integrative treatments, and procedural options (injections, radiofrequency ablation) can provide relief. Surgical options remain for end-stage disease.
